Vale woke standing upright inside a dead freight elevator, with no memory of entering it. A voice beneath the floor whispered closer, and the elevator doors shuddered open by themselves. He stepped out because the word felt less like an invitation than a countdown. The corridor beyond was tiled in wet black ceramic, each slab reflecting a different angle of his body. Thin red emergency tubes pulsed overhead like veins trapped behind smoked glass.

He followed a cable that crawled along the floor as though it could smell him. Every few steps, the voice repeated closer from a speaker farther ahead, never from the one nearest him. When he stopped, the cable curled around his boot and tugged once. The passage was narrow and impossibly long, fading into a purple haze without a visible end. Rusted service doors lined both walls, their number plates rearranging whenever the lights flickered.

Vale cut the cable with a folding blade, but both severed ends continued slithering in opposite directions. A low wobble rolled through the structure and made his teeth vibrate in a broken rhythm. He ran after the end disappearing into the haze before it could escape. The walls bowed inward like flexible metal, then snapped straight on each kick drum. Pale dust hung motionless in the air while everything around it seemed to jitter by single frames.

He reached a junction where six tunnels rotated around a central drain. The voice said closer through all six mouths at once, and each tunnel showed him running inside it. Vale chose the version in which his reflection was limping, though his own legs still felt whole. The junction floor was flooded with mirror-dark water that showed a ceiling full of stars. Green maintenance symbols glowed beneath the surface like drowned road signs.

Halfway down the chosen tunnel, Vale heard footsteps landing exactly between his own. He changed pace, but the second walker corrected instantly and stayed hidden behind the beat. He spun around and struck empty air, causing a human-shaped glitch to burst into squares and reform farther away. The tunnel was ribbed with concrete arches coated in silver condensation. Beyond them, the darkness had a granular texture, like old film burned into the shape of night.

The glitch figure began approaching without moving its legs. Vale backed away while its face cycled through strangers, animals, and blank loading screens. When the bass twisted upward, he threw his blade and watched the figure split around it like liquid software. Blue sparks drifted from exposed conduits and froze in suspended spirals. The concrete arches had become glossy and soft, resembling the inside of a mechanical throat.

A train arrived behind Vale even though no tracks had existed a second earlier. Its doors opened on a sharp snare, and the voice whispered closer from every empty seat. He boarded before the glitch figure could fold itself through the closing gap. The carriage was lit by sickly white panels that strobed in uneven groups of three. Black windows showed the same abandoned platform repeating outside, each copy smaller than the last.

The train accelerated, yet the repeated platform remained fixed beside him. Vale moved through the carriage and found passengers sitting perfectly still with their hands over their faces. When he touched one shoulder, every passenger lowered their hands and revealed Vale’s own expression. The seats were upholstered in dark green fabric patterned with tiny surveillance eyes. Chrome poles bent gently toward the rear, as if the whole carriage were melting from speed.

The copies stood together and began chanting closer in his voice. Vale shoved through them, but each body broke into horizontal strips and reassembled behind him. He reached the driver’s cabin and pulled the emergency lever until the rhythm abruptly dropped away. The front window contained only a vast tunnel filled with rotating white rings. Instrument panels glowed amber beneath layers of fingerprints that moved when he was not looking.

Silence held for one impossible second before the train struck something soft. Vale flew through the front window and landed in a street that had never been connected to the tunnel. The train passed through him as a cloud of black pixels and vanished into a wall. Tower blocks leaned over the road at crooked angles, their windows blinking in synchronized rows. Rain fell upward from the pavement into clouds the color of bruised copper.

Vale followed the road toward a distant antenna that flashed whenever the voice spoke. Each pulse of closer made parked vehicles unlock, start, and turn their headlights toward him. He walked faster as the cars began rolling backward in a slow mechanical pack. The street was carpeted with shattered screens displaying his silhouette from above. Shopfront mannequins stood behind dusty glass wearing cables instead of clothes.

The cars lunged when the drums returned, clipping through one another as they gained speed. Vale vaulted a barrier and slid into an alley just before the pack compressed into a single roaring machine. He kept moving while the fused vehicle scraped along the buildings, trying to squeeze itself after him. The alley walls were painted with fluorescent mouths, each one stretched into a silent scream. Pipes crossed overhead in dense knots, dripping luminous yellow fluid onto the bricks.

A service hatch opened beneath Vale’s hand before he touched it. He climbed down a ladder while the machine above hammered the alley into time with the bass. At the bottom, he found a room containing a single chair and a headset already playing his breathing. The chamber was circular and lined with gray acoustic foam shaped like sharpened teeth. A red bulb swung from the ceiling, dragging huge shadows around the walls.

Vale put on the headset and heard the voice from inside his left ear say closer. A map flashed across the visor, marking the antenna tower and a route that passed directly through solid earth. He tore the headset off, but the map remained burned across his vision. The room’s surfaces had no corners, only smooth curves disappearing into darkness. Hair-thin wires covered the floor in spirals around the chair like a metallic nest.

He followed the glowing route by pressing his palm against the wall. The wall opened around his fingers and let him walk through its concrete as if through cold smoke. Inside, he discovered a staircase descending sideways while gravity repeatedly changed its mind. The hidden space was filled with floating chunks of masonry suspended in violet fog. Far below, a white doorway hung alone with no building attached to it.

Vale crossed the sideways staircase by timing each step between gravity shifts. Every missed beat sent him falling toward a different surface, but the next kick returned him to the stairs. He reached the white doorway as the voice stretched the word closer into a metallic growl. The fog glittered with tiny black flakes that absorbed light instead of reflecting it. The doorway shone with clinical brightness, edged by scratches shaped like tally marks.

Beyond the door, technicians in transparent raincoats stood around an operating table. They turned toward Vale and froze, then skipped backward through their movements in perfect reverse. One technician pointed at the table, where a body under a sheet began matching Vale’s breathing. The laboratory was vast, with ceiling lamps vanishing into a haze of disinfectant mist. Glass tanks filled the walls, each containing a dark shape too folded to identify.

Vale pulled away the sheet and found an empty mold shaped precisely like him. The mold opened its eyes, spoke closer without moving its mouth, and grabbed his wrist. He broke free only after the beat glitched, repeating the same half-second until his arm occupied two places at once. The operating table was stained with iridescent oil that formed slow geometric patterns. Copper instruments hung above it like surgical tools designed for machines rather than flesh.

The technicians restarted and rushed him with blank plastic faces. Vale kicked the table into them, scattering bodies that shattered like hollow mannequins. He fled through the laboratory while the empty mold climbed upright and followed at walking speed. The glass tanks flashed with internal lightning, briefly revealing many versions of Vale curled inside. White floor tiles stretched toward every exit but bent upward near the walls like paper.

A sudden fake drop erased all sound and stopped every pursuer in place. Vale kept running through the silence, discovering that his footsteps produced no movement unless he imagined the missing beat. When the rhythm slammed back, he jumped forward through ten meters of space and crashed into an elevator lobby. The lobby was plated in black marble streaked with electric blue veins. Thirty elevator doors surrounded him in a perfect circle, each displaying the same descending number.

All thirty doors opened, revealing thirty versions of the same elevator. The voice whispered closer from the ceiling while the empty mold entered the lobby behind him. Vale chose the elevator whose mirrored back wall showed no reflection of either of them. The cabin was lined with tarnished brass engraved with waveforms and teeth. Its floor was transparent, exposing a shaft filled with slow-moving red clouds.

The elevator rose sideways through the tower and ignored every law Vale understood. The mold stood outside the closing doors, yet its hand remained inside and grew longer as the distance increased. Vale severed the stretching fingers with the emergency panel, and each one crawled into a separate corner. The shaft outside resembled a vertical city of cables, bridges, and hanging maintenance pods. Red clouds drifted between the structures like illuminated smoke underwater.

The severed fingers assembled themselves into a small animal with too many joints. It climbed Vale’s coat and pressed one fingertip against the map burned into his sight. Instead of attacking, it corrected the route and pointed toward a hatch in the elevator ceiling. The cabin lights flickered between crimson and ultraviolet, changing every material’s apparent texture. Condensation covered the brass walls in branching patterns like frost on a tomb.

Vale opened the hatch and pulled himself onto the moving elevator roof. The tower’s interior rushed past while the finger-creature balanced on his shoulder and clicked along with the hi-hats. He leaped onto a maintenance bridge just as the elevator vanished upward into a dark aperture. The bridge was a narrow strip of mesh suspended over an endless mechanical void. Thousands of tiny work lights blinked below in patterns resembling a sleeping nervous system.

Drones unfolded from the bridge railings and scanned Vale with flat green beams. He sprinted as their wings chopped the air into stuttering fragments and their warnings broke into corrupted syllables. The finger-creature jumped onto the nearest drone, rewired it with a touch, and sent it crashing through the others. The bridge twisted around a central column wrapped in cables as thick as tree trunks. Warning lights painted the metal in alternating bands of toxic green and deep red.

Vale reached the antenna chamber at the tower’s highest point. He inserted the finger-creature into a socket, and the entire room inhaled before projecting a colossal face above the city. The face looked like Vale viewed through damaged glass, and it mouthed closer while the tower answered with a bass growl. The chamber was an open circular platform surrounded by rotating antenna blades. Outside, the city spread beneath black clouds as a grid of wet neon wounds.

The projected face began descending from the sky toward the tower. Vale tried to disconnect the creature, but cables burst from the socket and stitched themselves into his sleeves. He realized the tower was not broadcasting the voice outward but calling something inward through him. The clouds formed a huge funnel lined with flickering digital noise. Every rooftop below carried a satellite dish turned toward the same point above Vale’s head.

The next drop hit, and the city folded upward around the tower like closing jaws. Vale tore free of the cables and climbed the central antenna as buildings rose vertically on every side. The descending face broke into millions of black birds that circled closer with each bass hit. Streets now stood like walls, covered in sideways traffic and streams of glowing rain. Windows surrounded him in all directions, each containing people frozen mid-scream.

Vale reached the antenna tip and found a second version of himself waiting there. The double removed its visor and revealed a smooth black surface where a face should have been. It pressed a hand to Vale’s chest, and the word closer came from inside his own ribs. The antenna tip was a thin silver needle above a rotating abyss of architecture. The flock formed a dark halo around both figures, threaded with brief flashes of white static.

Vale understood that every corridor, train, and tower had been built from his attempts to avoid this meeting. He stopped fighting and pulled the faceless double toward him instead. Their bodies collided, glitched apart, and recombined as one figure with two shadows moving independently. The city below shattered into floating rectangular layers like a dismantled video signal. Between those layers stretched a starless black depth crossed by luminous scan lines.

The merged figure opened its mouth, and the approaching voice finally emerged at full volume. Instead of saying closer, it issued a sound like a machine remembering fear, and every light in the city went dark. Vale felt the signal pass through him and continue toward someone beyond the world. The antenna chamber became a silhouette against a sky drained of color. Only the two shadows remained visible, crawling over empty air without a surface.

Darkness lasted until a wonky bass phrase bent the horizon into a grin. Vale stepped down from the antenna and walked across the air while his shadows ran ahead on invisible roads. With every playful glitch, pieces of the city snapped back in the wrong order and began dancing around him. Buildings floated upside down beneath a low ceiling of storm clouds. Neon signs hung without walls, spelling broken warnings in pink, cyan, and corpse-white light.

Vale entered the reconstructed street as the beat began stripping itself apart. The finger-creature returned from the tower, climbed into his palm, and curled into the shape of an ear. He listened through it and heard another heartbeat approaching from outside the city. The road was now perfectly clean and covered by a shallow sheet of silver water. Empty towers stood straight on both sides, their windows dark except for one moving light.

Vale turned toward that light and saw it growing larger inside every window at once. He raised the creature to his ear, and a new voice whispered closer in the exact tone of whoever was watching. Then he looked directly beyond the street, beyond the frame, and began walking toward the viewer. The city behind him stretched into a black tunnel ribbed with pulsing red geometry. In the foreground, his reflection arrived first, smiling from the wet pavement before his body reached it.
