A lone lighthouse keeper wakes at dawn on a windswept island. Amber light spills through a round window as gulls wheel over churning grey water. He pulls on a heavy wool coat and lights a small brass lantern.

He climbs the spiral stone staircase to the top of the tower, boots echoing. At the lamp room he wipes salt spray from the great lens and watches a fishing boat cut through the fog far below.

By midday the fog thickens and the boat's horn sounds three times, lost somewhere between the rocks. The keeper turns the lens to full brilliance and sweeps the beam across the sea, its light carving a golden path through the mist.

As evening falls the boat emerges safely into the harbour, its deck lamps glowing. The keeper leans on the rail, lantern at his side, and smiles as the first stars appear above the calm black water.
