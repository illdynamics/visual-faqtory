Six woke slumped in the driver's seat of a gutted OB van, no memory of climbing inside. A voice buried under the static kept repeating breaking, and it sounded closer with every loop. The van's rear doors kicked open on their own, so he climbed out because staying felt like waiting to be broadcast. The alley beyond was slicked with rain that mirrored a hundred dead screens bolted to the walls. Loose fibre cable coiled across the concrete like exposed nerve endings, twitching faintly under the sodium light.

He followed a fat trunk cable that pulsed along the gutter as if it could smell him. Every few steps the voice said breaking again from a speaker further down the alley, never the one closest to his ear. When he paused, the cable looped once around his ankle and pulled. The passage narrowed between two gutted studio blocks and dissolved into a haze of violet static with no visible end. Steel access doors lined both walls, their channel numbers rearranging themselves each time the streetlights flickered.

Six sliced the cable with a folding blade, but both severed ends kept thrashing like decapitated antennae. A low feedback wobble rolled through the walls and rattled his teeth in broken time. He chased the end sliding toward the haze before it could vanish completely. The walls flexed inward like sheet metal on every kick drum, then snapped back rigid. Dead pixels hung motionless in the air while everything else around them seemed to stutter by single frames.

He hit a junction where six studio corridors rotated slowly around a drain full of tape reels. The voice said breaking through all six speakers at once, and every corridor showed him already running down it. Six picked the version where his reflection limped, even though his own legs still felt fine. The junction floor was flooded ankle-deep with mirror-black water that reflected a ceiling of dead stars. Green ON AIR symbols glowed beneath the surface like drowned traffic lights.

Halfway down the chosen corridor, Six heard footsteps landing exactly between his own. He changed his pace and the second walker corrected instantly, staying hidden behind the beat. He spun and swung at empty air, and a camera-headed glitch burst into squares before reforming further off. The corridor was ribbed with cable trunking sweating silver condensation. Beyond it the dark had a grainy texture, like old broadcast tape burned into the shape of night.

The glitch figure came closer without moving its legs at all. Six backed off while its lens-shaped head cycled through news anchors, dead channels, and blank loading bars. When the bassline twisted upward he threw the blade, and the figure split around it like liquid signal. Blue sparks drifted out of a torn junction box and froze in slow spirals. The trunking behind it had gone glossy and soft, like the inside of a mechanical throat lined with wire.

An armoured newsvan roared in from nowhere, no road under its tyres. Its side doors slammed open on a hard snare hit, and the voice whispered breaking from every empty seat inside. Six climbed in before the glitch figure could fold itself through the closing gap. The cabin walls were monitor panels strobing in uneven groups of three. Every screen showed the same abandoned platform outside, each copy smaller than the last.

The van accelerated, but the platform outside stayed fixed at the same window. Six moved through the cabin and found a full crew sitting dead still with hands pressed over their faces. When he touched one shoulder, every crew member lowered their hands and showed him his own face. The seats were upholstered in dark fabric printed with tiny camera lenses. Chrome grab-poles bent gently toward the rear, as if the whole cabin were melting from speed.

The crew stood together and began chanting breaking in his own voice. Six shoved through them and each body broke into horizontal scan-lines, reassembling behind him. He reached the cab and yanked the kill switch until the rhythm dropped out completely. The windshield filled with nothing but a tunnel of rotating white noise rings. The dash glowed amber under layers of fingerprints that shifted whenever he stopped watching them.

Silence held for one impossible second before the van hit something soft. Six flew through the windshield and landed on a street that had never connected to any tunnel. The van passed through him as a burst of black pixels and vanished into a wall of screens. Tower blocks leaned over the road at crooked angles, every window blinking in synchronised rows. Rain fell upward off the pavement into clouds the colour of bruised copper.

Six followed the road toward a distant transmission mast that flashed red on every breaking. Each pulse made parked cars unlock, ignite, and swing their headlights toward him like camera flashes. He picked up his pace as the cars began rolling backward in a slow mechanical pack. The street was carpeted with shattered TV screens showing his own silhouette filmed from above. Shopfront mannequins stood behind dusty glass, wired up with cable instead of clothes.

The cars lunged the second the drums came back in, clipping through each other as they gained speed. Six vaulted a barrier and slid into an alley just before the pack fused into one roaring machine. He kept moving while the fused thing scraped along the buildings, trying to squeeze itself in after him. The alley walls were sprayed with fluorescent mouths stretched into silent screams. Pipes crossed overhead in dense knots, dripping toxic-green coolant onto the brick.

A service hatch popped open under Six's hand before he even touched it. He climbed down a ladder while the fused machine above hammered the alley into time with the sub bass. At the bottom he found a booth with one chair and a headset already looping his own breathing back at him. The booth was circular, lined with grey acoustic foam cut into rows of sharpened teeth. A red ON AIR bulb swung from the ceiling, dragging huge shadows around the walls.

Six put on the headset and heard the voice in his left ear say breaking. A map flashed across the visor, marking the transmission mast and a route that cut straight through solid concrete. He tore the headset off, but the map stayed burned across his vision anyway. The booth had no corners, just smooth curves disappearing into dark. Hair-thin fibre wires covered the floor in spirals around the chair like a metal nest.

He followed the burned-in route by pressing his palm flat against the wall. The wall dissolved into static under his fingers and let him walk straight through it like a dead channel. Inside, he found a staircase descending sideways while gravity kept changing its mind. The hidden space was packed with floating chunks of server rack suspended in violet fog. Far below, a white STANDBY doorway hung alone with no building attached to it.

Six crossed the sideways staircase by timing every step between gravity shifts. Every missed beat sent him falling toward a different surface, but the next kick always dropped him back on the stairs. He reached the white doorway just as the voice stretched breaking into a metallic growl. The fog glittered with tiny black flakes that swallowed light instead of reflecting it. The doorway shone with clinical brightness, edged in scratches shaped like countdown tally marks.

Beyond the door, technicians in clear rain slickers stood around an editing table. They turned toward Six, froze, then skipped backward through their own movements in perfect reverse. One pointed at the table, where a body under a sheet started matching his breathing exactly. The lab stretched out vast, its lamps vanishing into a haze of cooling mist. Glass tanks lined every wall, each holding a dark shape folded too tight to identify.

Six pulled the sheet away and found an empty shell moulded exactly like him. The shell opened its eyes, said breaking without moving its mouth, and grabbed his wrist. He broke free only when the beat glitched, repeating the same half-second until his arm existed in two places at once. The table underneath was stained with iridescent oil forming slow geometric patterns. Steel rigs hung above it, built for puppeteering machines rather than flesh.

The technicians restarted and rushed him with blank camera-lens faces. Six kicked the table into them, scattering bodies that shattered like hollow test dummies. He fled through the lab while the empty shell climbed upright and followed at a steady walking pace. The glass tanks flashed with internal static, briefly showing dozens of versions of Six curled inside. White floor tiles stretched toward every exit but curled upward near the walls like burnt paper.

A sudden fake drop wiped out all sound and froze every pursuer in place. Six kept running through the silence, finding his footsteps produced no movement unless he imagined the missing beat himself. When the rhythm slammed back in, he jumped ten metres forward and crashed straight into a server lobby. The lobby was tiled in black marble streaked with glowing blue circuit veins. Thirty lift doors ringed the room in a perfect circle, each showing the same descending signal bars.

All thirty doors opened at once, revealing thirty identical lifts. The voice whispered breaking from the ceiling speakers while the shell entered the lobby behind him. Six chose the one lift whose mirrored back wall reflected neither of them. The cabin was lined in scratched steel engraved with waveforms and rows of teeth. Its floor was transparent, showing a shaft filled with slow-drifting red static clouds.

The lift rose sideways through the tower, ignoring every law Six understood. The shell stood outside the closing doors, but its hand stayed inside and kept stretching as the gap grew. Six severed the stretching fingers with the emergency panel, and each one crawled off into a separate corner. The shaft outside looked like a vertical data-centre city of cable bridges and hanging server pods. Red static clouds drifted between the structures like illuminated smoke underwater.

The severed fingers pulled themselves together into a small creature with too many joints and a lens for an eye. It climbed Six's coat and pressed one fingertip against the map burned into his sight. Instead of attacking, it corrected the route and pointed toward a hatch in the lift's ceiling. The cabin lights flickered between crimson and ultraviolet, changing the texture of everything they touched. Condensation covered the steel walls in branching patterns like frost creeping over a server rack.

Six opened the hatch and hauled himself onto the moving roof of the lift. The tower's interior rushed past while the little creature balanced on his shoulder, clicking along with the hi-hats. He leapt onto a maintenance bridge just as the lift vanished upward into a dark port. The bridge was a narrow strip of cable mesh strung over an endless mechanical void. Thousands of tiny status lights blinked below in patterns like a sleeping nervous system.

Drones unfolded from the bridge railings and scanned Six with flat green beams. He sprinted as their rotors chopped the air into stuttering fragments and their warnings broke into corrupted syllables. The little creature leapt onto the nearest drone, rewired it with a touch, and sent it crashing through the others. The bridge twisted around a central column wrapped in cables as thick as tree trunks. Warning lights painted the metal in alternating bands of toxic green and blood red.

Six reached the transmission spire at the tower's highest point. He plugged the creature into the mast socket, and the whole structure inhaled before projecting a colossal broadcaster's face above the city. The face looked like Six viewed through cracked glass, mouthing breaking while the tower answered with a sub-bass growl. The chamber was an open circular platform ringed by slowly rotating antenna blades. Below, the city spread out under black cloud, riot fires and neon glowing like wet wounds.

The projected face began descending from the sky toward the tower. Six tried to yank the creature free, but cables burst from the socket and stitched themselves straight into his sleeves. He realised the tower had never been broadcasting outward, only calling something inward through him the whole time. The clouds formed one huge funnel lined with flickering digital noise. Every rooftop dish and every riot drone below had turned to point at the exact same spot above his head.

The next drop hit, and the city folded upward around the tower like closing jaws. Six tore free of the cables and climbed the central mast as buildings rose vertically on every side. The descending face shattered into a screaming flock of pixel-crows built from broken antennae, circling closer with every bass hit. The streets now stood like walls, covered in sideways traffic and streams of glowing embers. Screens on every window showed the crowd below, faces frozen mid-scream.

Six reached the mast's tip and found a second version of himself already waiting there. The double pulled off its headset and revealed a smooth lens where a face should have been. It pressed one hand to his chest, and the word breaking came from somewhere inside his own ribs. The mast tip was a thin silver needle above a rotating abyss of architecture. The flock formed a dark halo around both of them, threaded with brief flashes of white static.

Six understood that every corridor, van, and tower had been built out of his own attempts to dodge this exact meeting. He stopped fighting and pulled the faceless double toward him instead. Their bodies collided, glitched apart, and recombined into one figure trailing two independent shadows. The city below shattered into floating rectangular layers, like a dismantled video signal. Between the layers stretched a starless black depth crossed by luminous scan lines.

The merged figure opened its mouth, and the approaching voice finally hit full volume. Instead of breaking, it let out a sound like a transmitter finally running out of signal, and every light in the city died at once. Six felt the broadcast pass straight through him and keep going, toward whoever was still watching beyond the frame. The spire became a silhouette against a sky drained of all colour. Only the two shadows stayed visible, crawling across empty air with no surface under them.

Darkness held until a wonky bass phrase bent the horizon into a grin. Six stepped down from the mast and walked across open air while his shadows ran ahead on invisible roads. With every playful glitch, pieces of the city snapped back in the wrong order and started dancing around him. Buildings floated upside down beneath a low ceiling of storm cloud. Broken neon spelled out NO SIGNAL and SYSTEM OVERLOAD in pink, cyan, and corpse-white light, hanging with no walls to hold them up.

Six walked into the reconstructed street as the beat began stripping itself apart layer by layer. The little creature came back, climbed into his palm, and curled itself into the shape of an ear. He listened through it and heard another signal, a second heartbeat, approaching from somewhere outside the city. The road ahead was clean, covered by a shallow sheet of water that shone like static made liquid. Empty towers stood straight on both sides, every window dark except for one light on the move.

Six turned toward that light and watched it grow larger inside every window at once. He raised the creature to his ear, and a new voice whispered breaking in the exact tone of whoever was watching. Then he looked straight past the street, past the frame itself, and started walking toward the viewer. The city behind him stretched into a black tunnel ribbed with pulsing red geometry. In the foreground, his reflection arrived first, smiling up from the wet pavement before his body ever caught up.
